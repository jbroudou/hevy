# Detraining — State of Play

**As of Thu 17 Sep 2026.** The question: *how do we slow the rate of athletic
decay when not training?* A standing goal, not tied to the November bout — it
governs travel, illness, work pressure, injury layoffs and the post-fight
break.
Plan: [2026-09-17-minimum-dose-maintenance-plan.md](2026-09-17-minimum-dose-maintenance-plan.md).

Basis tags: **[C]** COROS · **[H]** Hevy · **[L]** literature, read this
session · **[A]** athlete · **[I]** inference.

## Bottom line

- **The answer to the question is: keep the intensity, cut the frequency and
  the volume.** Two hard cardio sessions a week and one heavy strength session
  a week hold almost everything, for months. Six easy sessions a week hold
  much less than two hard short ones. [L]
- **The decay risk is asymmetric, and it lands exactly where this athlete's
  habit is weakest.** Aerobic fitness starts falling within about a week;
  strength survives weeks of nothing at all. And the record shows strength
  training that almost never stops alongside cardio that stops constantly. [C,
  H, L]
- **Recorded cardio, 1 Jan – 23 Aug: 15 sessions in 234 days.** Median gap
  between sessions **14 days**; 8 of the 15 gaps were 14 days or longer; the
  longest was **39 days** (5 Jun → 14 Jul), with 30 and 29-day gaps either side
  of it. [C]
- **Strength over the same period barely gapped at all: 100 sessions on 90
  distinct days since 30 Nov 2025, longest break 12 days.** [H]
- **This is not what the five-run decline is.** That is a session-execution
  problem, documented in
  [../vo2-recovery/README.md](../vo2-recovery/README.md). Detraining is a
  separate failure mode with separate countermeasures. Don't treat one as
  evidence about the other. [I]
- **Nothing is currently measured that would detect decay early.** COROS
  VO2max has read 41 throughout and is too coarse; no repeatable benchmark
  exists. [C]

## The athlete's own layoff record

Recorded cardio sessions on the watch, 1 Jan – 23 Aug 2026, i.e. everything
before the camp block began on 24 Aug. Gaps in days between consecutive
sessions: [C]

| From → to | Gap |
| --- | --- |
| 5 Jun → 14 Jul | **39** |
| 21 Feb → 23 Mar | **30** |
| 16 Jul → 14 Aug | **29** |
| 24 Apr → 13 May | 19 |
| 23 Mar → 10 Apr | 18 |
| 6 Feb → 20 Feb, 10 Apr → 24 Apr, 22 May → 5 Jun | 14 each |
| the remaining six gaps | 1–11 |

Set against the decay table in the plan, the three longest gaps sit in the
range where **mitochondrial capacity, not plasma volume, is what is being
lost** — citrate synthase is already down ~29% by day 10, ~40% by day 56. The
39-day gap is at roughly the −10 to −13% VO2max mark in the published time
courses. [L, I]

Strength, by contrast, was never interrupted for more than 12 days (13 → 25 Aug
2026, and 30 Dec 2025 → 11 Jan 2026), at 5–14 sessions a month throughout. On
the published decay rates, **no strength gap in this record was long enough to
cost anything measurable** — the threshold is about 3 weeks. [H, L]

*Caveat:* COROS sees only what is recorded on the watch. Boxing is recorded
nowhere, and gym cardio has never been logged, so real inactivity may be
shorter than these gaps suggest. The gaps are reliable as a record of
*structured, intensity-controlled* cardio; not as proof of doing nothing. [C, I]

## What decays, and how fast [L]

Full sourcing in the plan. In brief:

| Quality | Survives | Then falls at |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Blood / plasma volume | days | −5% at 14 days; restored within days of restarting |
| Mitochondrial enzymes | days | −29% at 10 days, −40% at 56 days |
| VO2max | ~1 week | −4 to −7% at 2 weeks, −10% at 5 weeks, −13% at 8 weeks, ~−18% at 12 weeks |
| Power | ~3 weeks | 0.1–0.6% per week |
| Strength | ~3 weeks | ~2% per week; largely retained through 32 weeks in one trial |
| Muscle size | weeks | Held by 1 session/week in 20–35s; **not** held in 60–75s |
| Skill / boxing technique | unknown | **No evidence base, and no data here** |

At 46 there is a second clock running underneath: masters endurance athletes
who hold their training volume lose 5–6.5% of VO2max per decade, those who cut
it by more than 20% lose 15–46% per decade. Twelve weeks of complete cessation
costs about as much as a decade of ageing. [L]

## Current prescription [L, I]

Two modes. Neither replaces the camp prescriptions — they apply when those
can't be met.

**Reduced time (~2.5 h/week):** two hard cardio sessions at unchanged
intensity (the 4×4 bike session, work 161–170 bpm — cut warm-up and filler,
never reps); one full-body strength session, 5–6 exercises, 2–3 sets, **loads
unchanged**, 3–4 reps in reserve; ≥7,000 steps on non-training days. A second
strength session is preferred at this age for muscle size, though that rests on
a judgement about age brackets, not a finding. [I]

**Short breaks (under a week):** nothing is at risk and the correct dose is
none. The earliest measured losses anywhere in the evidence start at day 6
(mitochondrial enzymes) and day 12 (VO2max); strength holds ~3 weeks. The risk
in a short break is behavioural, not physiological — four days becoming four
weeks. Book the session that ends the break before the break starts, and spend
the window on the two things that keep getting skipped: shoulder rehab and the
calf/soleus routine.

**Between sessions:** step reduction is a loss stimulus in its own right. A
walking pad is the only desk option that moves the step count (~+2,000/day
observed; a standing desk adds ~9 kcal/hour and no steps) — but it is NEAT, not
a session: at ~90–105 bpm it cannot be logged as the easy Zone 2 session, and
the jump in cumulative calf load needs ramping at 91 kg.

**Complete stop:** ≤7 days costs nothing worth counting; 8–14 days costs mostly
plasma volume and comes back within days; beyond 14 days the losses stop being
cheap. **Hard rule: no cardio gap longer than 14 days, target 7.** A deliberate
7–14 day stop after the fight is worth taking, followed by the maintenance week
rather than by nothing. Coming back: restore intensity early, volume last —
capped at half the pre-layoff volume in week one, because the calf and Achilles
tolerance decays faster than the will to run.

## What this goal can get wrong

- **Running maintenance mode while normal training is possible would
  under-train.** These are floors, not targets. [I]
- **The dosing evidence is mostly general-population, not athletes.** Spiering
  says so himself and declines to make athlete-specific recommendations; for
  highly trained people other authors suggest cutting frequency by no more than
  ~20%, which is far more conservative than the 6 → 2 finding. [L]
- **The age evidence has a hole exactly where this athlete sits.** The dosing
  trial used 20–35 and 60–75 year olds. Applying the older group's rule at 46
  is inference. [L, I]
- **Skill decay is invisible here.** No literature in this set covers punch
  timing or defensive reflex, and boxing is recorded in neither system. [I]
