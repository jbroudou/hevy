## Rules that decide the dose

1. **Stop before fatigue.** Three sharp jumps beat twenty tired ones. If jumps
   get slower or landings get noisy, the set is over. [PDF]
2. **Not HIIT.** Rest is the point — take the full rest even when it feels
   unnecessary. Conditioning is Monday and Wednesday. [PDF]
3. **No punches, including phantom ones.** Footwork is stance, step, pivot,
   angle. The 17 Sep arms-free advice stands. [shoulder-rehab plan]
4. **No upper-body load.** Hands on hips or a light arm swing for the jumps;
   dumbbells for the lunges hang at the side, and are dropped if the shoulder
   objects. Sled push was left out for this reason.
5. **Ground-contact budget.** Session A is roughly 70 high-intensity contacts
   plus ~75 low-intensity pogo contacts; Session B roughly 40 higher-intensity
   contacts plus ~150 low-intensity hops. That sits at the low end of published
   beginner–intermediate volumes and was chosen for a 91 kg athlete with a
   documented calf deficit, not for a trained jumper. [I]
6. **Sharp pain in the Achilles, shin or knee = stop.** Burning fatigue is
   expected in the calf block; sharp or localised pain is not. [PDF, calf plan]

## How to do each exercise

One demo video per exercise. Links checked 21 Sep 2026 via YouTube oEmbed (the
title and channel match); the videos themselves were not reviewed. Where a video
differs from this plan, the text here wins. The same content is on pages 3–5 of

### Plyo A – Power

**Pogo Jumps**

1. Stand tall, feet hip-width, hands on hips.
2. Bounce straight up and down off the balls of the feet. Knees stay almost straight — the spring comes from the ankles.
3. Heels never touch the floor. Each contact should be short and quiet, like the floor is hot.
4. Height is only a few centimetres. Rhythm and stiffness, not effort.

*Watch for:* Knees bending more with each hop, heels sinking, or landings getting loud — end the set.

Video: [Pogo Jumps Tutorial – Proper Form and Technique](https://youtu.be/j0nl5dWuqN4) (Runna)

**Countermovement Jump (Hevy: Jump Squat)**

1. Stand feet hip- to shoulder-width, hands on hips (arms stay out of it this week).
2. Dip quickly to about a quarter squat — hips back, chest up — and without pausing at the bottom, jump as high as you can.
3. Land on the balls of the feet rolling to the whole foot, knees bent and pointing over the toes, and hold the landing still.
4. Stand up, reset, then do the next rep. Each rep is a single maximal jump, not a bounce.

*Watch for:* Knees caving inward on the take-off or landing.

Video: [How To Do a Counter Movement Jump](https://youtu.be/sLvArswhYMQ) (Swift Movement Academy)

*The video may use an arm swing; keep hands on hips this week.*

**Split-Stance Jump**

1. Take your boxing stance: feet about shoulder-width apart, lead foot forward, rear heel slightly up.
2. Dip slightly and jump straight up as high as you can from that stance.
3. Land in the same stance — same width, same foot forward. Stick it for a second.
4. 4 reps in orthodox stance, then switch to southpaw for 4.

*Watch for:* Feet landing in a line (narrowed stance) or crossing. Keep the width you'd fight in.

Video: [Staggered Stance Vertical Jump](https://youtu.be/-s_G3h98s5s) (THIRSTgym.com / Brandon Smitley)

*Shows a staggered (front–back) stance. Use your wider boxing stance and keep your feet the same on landing.*

**Broad Jump (stick the landing)**

1. Stand feet hip-width behind a line, hands on hips or a small arm swing.
2. Dip hips back and jump forward as far as you can.
3. Land on both feet, knees bent over toes, hips back — and freeze. Hold 2 seconds.
4. Walk back to the line. No rebound into a second jump.

*Watch for:* Falling forward or stepping out of the landing. If you can't hold it, you jumped further than you can control. Shorten the jump.

Video: [Broad Jump w/stick](https://youtu.be/bHT0w3KADFI) (Bullett Performance Training)

**Lateral Bounds**

1. Stand on your right leg, knee soft, hips slightly back.
2. Push hard off the right leg and bound sideways to the left.
3. Land on your left leg only, softly, knee bent over the toes, hips back. Control it for a moment.
4. Push off the left leg and bound back to the right. That's 1 rep per side.

*Watch for:* Knee collapsing inward on the landing leg, or the trailing foot touching down to save the landing.

Video: [How To Lateral Bound](https://youtu.be/soqQy4dzEts) (Third Space London)

**Boxing Footwork: step in → angle out**

1. Start in stance, guard relaxed, hands low if the shoulder objects.
2. Explode forward: push off the rear foot, lead foot steps in, rear foot follows the same distance.
3. Immediately exit on an angle: pivot on the lead foot, swinging the rear foot round about 90°. Or L-step out (see Plyo B).
4. Reset to a balanced stance. That's one burst — repeat for the 15 seconds.

*Watch for:* Crossing the feet, or ending the pivot square-on and flat-footed. You should finish ready to move again.

Video: [Boxing Footwork: Step Out and Pivot](https://youtu.be/TS0eQ9ytkVE) (KingFit Training)

*Ignore any punching in the video. No punches this week.*

**Walking Lunge (Dumbbell)**

1. A 20 kg dumbbell in each hand, arms hanging straight at your sides. Or go bodyweight.
2. Take a long step forward and lower until the back knee almost touches the floor. The front shin stays roughly vertical.
3. Drive through the front heel to stand and bring the back foot through into the next step.
4. Torso upright the whole way, 20 m per set.

*Watch for:* Front knee caving in, or leaning the torso forward. If carrying the dumbbells bothers the shoulder, drop them.

Video: [Dumbbell Walking Lunge – How To](https://youtu.be/I34ysEkPK7w) (Bobby Maximus)

### Plyo B – Reactive Feet

**Alternating Plate Pogo**

1. Put a weight plate (or a low step or line) on the floor in front of you.
2. Hop on one foot, then the other, in a quick rhythm, like running on the spot with springy ankles. Tap the balls of the feet onto the plate alternately.
3. Stay on the balls of the feet, knees nearly straight, contacts short and light.
4. Keep a steady rhythm for the full 20 seconds.

*Watch for:* Losing the rhythm or landing flat-footed. Slow down until the rhythm is clean.

Video: [Alternating Pogo Hops](https://youtu.be/UMkV8sT2lvw) (Relentless Athletics)

*This video shows the drill on flat ground. Ted Ryce's plate version is in the videos in his original thread: x.com/ted_ryce/status/2100563272713846966*

**Line Hops (forward/back, then side/side)**

1. Put a strip of tape or a line on the floor. Stand with both feet together just behind it.
2. Sets 1–3: hop forward over the line and straight back, as fast as you can, both feet together.
3. Sets 4–6: stand beside the line and hop side to side over it.
4. Keep the hops low, stay on the balls of the feet with stiff ankles, and spend as little time on the ground as possible.

*Watch for:* Hops getting higher and slower as you tire. Low and fast is the point.

Video: [Line Hop Forward Backward](https://youtu.be/622hqFSCzls) (YST Exercises)

*Side to side: youtu.be/S1aRerlNobs (Line Hops – Side to Side, Horton Barbell)*

**Skater Bound → stick (Hevy: Lateral Bounds)**

1. Same movement as Tuesday's lateral bound, but the landing is the drill.
2. Bound sideways off one leg and land on the other.
3. Freeze on one leg for a full 2 seconds: hips back, knee over toes, trailing foot off the floor.
4. Only then bound back the other way. Distance doesn't matter. The quiet, balanced stop does.

*Watch for:* Wobbling or the trailing foot tapping down. Make the bound shorter until the stick is solid.

Video: [Lateral Bound with Stick](https://youtu.be/XDBHOQoAa3w) (Champion Physical Therapy and Performance)

**Boxing Footwork: stance shuffle**

1. Start in stance. Forward: push off the rear foot, lead foot steps, rear foot follows (step-drag). Then back: push off the lead foot, rear foot steps, lead foot follows.
2. Sideways: to go left, the left foot moves first. To go right, the right foot moves first. Then return.
3. Every step is short and explosive, and the stance width is the same after each movement.
4. 10-second bursts, full reset between.

*Watch for:* Feet coming together or crossing, or bouncing high. Glide low instead.

Video: [Boxing Footwork Technique #1 – Step-drag](https://youtu.be/dHUutXudf8o) (expertboxing)

**Boxing Footwork: L-step / angle-out**

1. L-step (orthodox): from stance, step the rear (right) foot back, then step the lead (left) foot across to the right. Your feet trace an L and you end up facing a new angle. Southpaw is the mirror image.
2. Chain it: step in → L-step or pivot out → forward → back → lateral angle.
3. Stay low throughout. Accelerate, brake, redirect.
4. Finish every movement in stance, ready to move again.

*Watch for:* Standing tall as you change direction, or ending with the feet in a line.

Video: [L-Step Tutorial – Boxing Footwork](https://youtu.be/0oHwImE_nRQ) (McLeod Scott Boxing)

*Ignore any punching in the video. No punches this week.*

**Seated Calf Raise**

1. Sit in the seated calf machine with the pad on your lower thighs, knees at about 90°, balls of the feet on the platform edge.
2. Lower the heels as far as they go for a full stretch.
3. Rise onto the toes over 2 seconds, pause 1 second at the top, and lower over 3 seconds.
4. No machine? Sit on a bench with the balls of your feet on a plate and a dumbbell on each knee.

*Watch for:* Bouncing out of the bottom or cutting the range short. The slow full range is the exercise.

Video: [Seated Calf Raise – Proper Form Tutorial](https://youtu.be/I1uQtobaNRQ) (FIT.nl)

**Isometric Calf Hold**

1. Stand on a step on the balls of both feet, heels hanging off, lightly holding a rail for balance only.
2. Rise up onto your toes as high as you can.
3. Hold that top position for 30 seconds.
4. The set is over when the heels start to sink, even if the clock hasn't finished.

*Watch for:* Heels drifting down while you keep counting. Height is the whole exercise.

Video: [How To Do An Isometric Calf Hold](https://youtu.be/gSxLKbjO26A) (Footballing Journal)

**Tibialis Raise**

1. Lean your back against a wall with your feet about a step or two out in front, heels on the floor.
2. Pull your toes and the front of your feet up toward your shins as far as they go.
3. Lower slowly until your toes almost touch the floor, then raise again.
4. The further your feet are from the wall, the harder it gets. Start close.

*Watch for:* Rocking your body to lift the toes. Keep your back against the wall and let the shins do the work.

Video: [Tibialis Wall Raises (Exercise Demo)](https://youtu.be/VzIcGAgBiaM) (The Barefoot Sprinter)
