# Rapid Return-to-Boxing Plan

**Prepared 15 Sep 2026.** This plan is based on the history in this folder and
current clinical guidance. It is not a diagnosis.

## Recommendation

Book a sports physiotherapist this week and begin the short rehabilitation
session below now. That is the quickest evidence-based route back to boxing.
Light, controlled punching can start when the entry criteria are met; hard
sparring cannot.

Do not use BPC-157, TB-500 or a similar "healing peptide". There is no human
evidence that either heals a shoulder tendon faster, both are unapproved in
Australia, and both are prohibited in sport at all times.

## What the history does and does not show

The current working explanation remains **rotator-cuff-related shoulder pain**:
pain started after a sharp increase in sparring, missing right crosses was the
main trigger, the right side is worse, and some arm positions are comfortable
while others cause 5–6/10 pain.

That explanation is plausible, but it has **not been confirmed**. The movement
tests in the existing plan cannot rule out a partial cuff tear, biceps or labral
pain, instability, neck referral, or another cause. Warming up and painless
clicking also do not rule those conditions out.

The main practical problem is not failure of rehabilitation. It is that an
adequate course has not happened: only two sessions are recorded in the 20 days
since 26 Aug, the second omitted most of the program, and no pain response was
recorded. The
full program also took about 49 minutes, which made adherence less likely.

## The quickest safe path

### In the next 2–3 days

1. **Book a sports physiotherapist.** Ask for:
   - active and passive range of motion;
   - external- and internal-rotation and scaption strength measured with a
     handheld dynamometer;
   - assessment of the cuff, biceps/labrum, instability, neck and nerves;
   - a boxing-specific return test and review after 10–14 days.
2. **Do not spar or throw hard punches.** Also pause any press, dip, fly,
   upright row or lateral raise that reproduces the pain. Lower-body training,
   running, cycling and genuinely pain-free upper-body work can continue.
3. **Stop repeatedly provoking the 6/10 thumb-neutral raise.** A painful test
   is not treatment. Record symptoms during ordinary movement instead.
4. Record a baseline **SPADI** (Shoulder Pain and Disability Index), then repeat
   it weekly. It measures whether pain and function are actually improving.

Imaging is not automatically the first step. Current guidance recommends a
clinical assessment first. If the examination finds true weakness, instability
or another concerning feature, or if an appropriate rehabilitation course is
not producing improvement, diagnostic ultrasound is usually the first cuff
test; MRI is reserved for questions ultrasound cannot answer or for surgical
planning.

### The daily record

Use one line per day:

`rest pain / normal arm-raise pain / night pain / rehab or boxing load / pain during / pain next morning / painkiller use`

For boxing, also record the number of right crosses, approximate intensity,
and number of misses. Duration alone hides the load that originally caused the
problem.

## Short rehabilitation session

Perform this on **three non-consecutive days per week**. Two sessions is the
minimum. Keep it to about 20–25 minutes so it actually gets done. A physio may
change the selection after examining the shoulder.

| Exercise | Starting dose | Main rule |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Cable or band external rotation, elbow at side | 3 × 12–15 per side | Slow 3-second return; finish with 3–4 clean reps in reserve |
| Thumb-up scaption | 3 × 10–15 | Use a pain-free range; slow 3-second lowering |
| Cable or band row | 3 × 12–15 | No shrugging; do not train to failure |
| Serratus punch or wall slide | 2 × 12–15 | Smooth shoulder-blade movement |
| Cable or band punch-deceleration | 2 × 8 per side | 3–4 seconds toward extension; soft elbow; omit if it causes more than 2/10 pain, catching or instability |

External rotation at shoulder height and the bottoms-up carry are useful later,
but they are not mandatory in week one. Add external rotation at shoulder
height at 2 × 10–12 once that position is comfortable. Add the carry only if
the session still stays short.

### Loading rules

- Aim for **0–2/10 pain**. A stable 3/10 is a hold signal, not a progression
  signal. Sharp pain, a painful catch, instability or new weakness means stop
  that exercise.
- Symptoms must return to the pre-session level within 24 hours. The next
  morning should be the same or better.
- After two passed sessions, add 1–2 repetitions. At the top of the range, add
  the smallest available resistance and return to the bottom of the range.
- For the deceleration drill, progress in this order: control, repetitions,
  resistance, then speed. Change only one variable at a time.
- If next-morning pain rises by 2 or more points, or remains elevated beyond
  24 hours, reduce the provoking exercise or boxing dose by about 25–30%.
- Do not use pain relief to make a failed load test look like a pass. Record
  the score before medication and do not increase training merely because the
  medication reduced pain.

## Return-to-boxing ladder

This is a progression from **return to participation** to **return to full
performance**. Starting controlled boxing does not mean the shoulder is ready
for unrestricted sparring.

### Entry gate for light punching

Start rung 1 only when all of these are true:

- rest and ordinary daily movement are 0–2/10;
- there is no painful catching, instability, new weakness or worsening night
  pain;
- the arm moves comfortably through the range needed for straight punches;
- two rehabilitation sessions, at least 48 hours apart, caused no next-morning
  increase.

Footwork, head movement and lower-body conditioning without punching can start
before this gate if they do not irritate the shoulder.

### Progression

Complete **two sessions at each rung**, at least 48 hours apart, with symptoms
back to baseline by the next morning. Increase only one of punch count,
intensity, round length or exposure to self-braked misses at a time.

| Rung | Boxing exposure |
| --- | --- |
| 1 | 2 × 2 min easy shadowboxing at 30–40%; short, relaxed straight punches; no snapping into end range |
| 2 | 3 × 2 min shadowboxing at 50–60%; add controlled right crosses and normal combinations |
| 3 | 3 × 2 min bag or pads at 50–60%; punches are placed to land; coach checks that the rear hand is not overreaching |
| 4 | 4 × 3 min bag or pads at 70–80%; add a small number of controlled self-braked crosses in shadowboxing |
| 5 | 5 × 3 min bag or pads at 80–90%; normal combinations and fatigue, but no sparring |
| 6 | 2–3 technical sparring rounds at no more than 50%; no hard rear hands; add one round or about 10% intensity only after two passes |

If a rung fails, return to the last passed rung. Do not push through and then
take several days off; that produces less useful training than a smaller dose
that can be repeated.

### Gate for sparring

Because boxing is both a throwing and contact sport, unrestricted sparring
requires more than low pain during one session. Before rung 6, a physio should
confirm:

- pain-free boxing-specific range of motion;
- no apprehension, catching or instability;
- external rotation, internal rotation and scaption strength and endurance
  close to the other side and adequate in absolute terms; **90% limb symmetry
  is a practical target, not a universal rule**, especially because the left
  shoulder was also mildly affected;
- a passed upper-limb functional test chosen for the athlete, such as the
  closed-chain upper-extremity stability test;
- two high-level non-contact boxing sessions completed without symptoms during,
  after or the next morning.

Unrestricted competition requires two high-level contact sessions as well as
the ability to complete the intended 5 × 3-minute demand without pain or loss
of mechanics. A calendar date alone is not a clearance test.

## Treatment options, ranked by usefulness now

| Option | Recommendation | Practical consequence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Clinical assessment plus progressive exercise | **Do now** | Best-supported route; confirms what should be loaded and prevents losing time to the wrong diagnosis |
| Load modification and graded boxing | **Do now** | Preserves fitness and skill while rebuilding the exact tolerance that failed |
| Topical/oral NSAID or paracetamol | **Optional short-term symptom relief after pharmacist/doctor screening** | May make sleep and rehabilitation easier, but does not prove healing; oral NSAIDs have stomach, kidney and cardiovascular risks |
| Manual therapy or taping | **Optional adjunct** | Can reduce pain briefly; it does not replace progressive loading |
| One ultrasound-guided corticosteroid injection | **Not first-line; discuss only if pain prevents sleep or rehabilitation** | Can improve pain in the short term but has no demonstrated long-term benefit; it must not be used to rush back through masked pain |
| PRP injection | **Not now** | Evidence is inconsistent and AAOS does not support routine use for cuff tendinopathy or partial tears; it is costly and not a reliable shortcut |
| Shockwave or calcific lavage | **Only if imaging confirms calcific tendinopathy** | Shockwave is not recommended for ordinary non-calcific cuff pain |
| Surgery | **No current indication from this history** | Relevant only if examination/imaging finds a repairable structural problem or good nonsurgical care fails |
| BPC-157 or TB-500 | **Do not use** | No demonstrated human shoulder/tendon benefit, unknown safety and product quality, injection risk, and an anti-doping violation risk |

Medication choice depends on medical history, including kidney disease,
ulcers, heart disease, blood-pressure treatment and anticoagulants. A
pharmacist or doctor should check that rather than this plan guessing.

## Peptides: the direct answer

### BPC-157

The FDA's 2026 review found only five small, short human studies across bowel,
bladder, knee-pain and early safety uses. None establishes that BPC-157 heals a
shoulder or tendon injury. The FDA found insufficient clinical safety data and
specific concerns about impurities, aggregation and immune reactions in
injectable products.

### TB-500

TB-500 is a fragment related to thymosin beta-4. Results from cells, rodents,
or full-length thymosin beta-4 products do not establish that TB-500 heals a
human tendon. The FDA states that it has not identified human exposure data for
the TB-500 fragment.

### Australian and sporting status

The TGA lists BPC-157 and TB-500 as unapproved peptide products. It warns that
online or compounded products may have unreliable ingredients or doses and may
cause contamination, infection, local tissue damage, allergic reactions or
other immediate and long-term harm.

Under the 2026 WADA list, BPC-157 is prohibited under S0 and TB-500/thymosin
beta-4 under S2.3. Both are prohibited in and out of competition. If the bout
is governed by a WADA-compliant policy, using them could end the return before
the shoulder does.

The expected benefit is unknown while the medical, product-quality and
anti-doping downsides are real. That makes the risk-to-benefit decision clear:
do not use them.

## Expected timing

There is no honest guaranteed date without an examination. If this is
uncomplicated rotator-cuff-related pain and the short program is completed
consistently, light shadowboxing may be possible once the entry gate is passed,
often well before unrestricted sparring. The 1 Nov fight date is only 6.7 weeks
away; that may be too soon for a full, tested return. The later November dates
leave more room, but should still be decided by the gates above rather than by
the calendar.

## Get urgent assessment sooner if

- the arm suddenly cannot be raised;
- there is new or worsening objective weakness;
- the shoulder dislocates, feels unstable, or locks painfully;
- there is deformity, major swelling, redness, fever or a new traumatic event;
- numbness or tingling persists;
- severe rest or night pain is worsening; or
- chest pain, breathlessness or other non-shoulder symptoms accompany it.
