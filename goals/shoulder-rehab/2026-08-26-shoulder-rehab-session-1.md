## What went well

**The slow eccentrics appear to have been genuinely performed.** Modelling
working time from the plan's own tempos (1s out / 3s back on ER, 2s up / 3s
down on scaption, 3–4s out on the deceleration drill), across both sides where
the plan says "per side", plus the prescribed rests, predicts a **46-minute**
session. Actual was **49 minutes**. A rushed session with 1-second reps would
have come in near 25 minutes. The duration is the strongest available evidence
that tempo discipline and per-side work held up — worth noting given no RPE or
notes were logged.

**Exercise 1 was upgraded from a band to a cable.** This is a defensible
improvement over the written plan. A band's tension *falls* as it shortens, so
on the slow 3-second return the resistance drops away exactly where the plan
wants eccentric load. A cable holds tension throughout, and is measurable in kg
so progression can be tracked. The plan's intent is better served by the cable.

**Exercise 5 was performed, not skipped.** The plan says to skip external
rotation at 90° if the position reproduces current pain. It was completed at
full prescribed reps, which — absent contrary notes — suggests the position was
tolerated. This is meaningful: it is the more athletic, more boxing-relevant
cuff position.

**Exercise 4 shows the documented warm-up pattern.** Set 1 fell short at 2 kg
(13 reps), dropped to 1 kg, then completed 15 reps at 2 kg on set 3. That
matches the plan's own observation that the shoulders "feel worse when first
moving, then ease as they warm up."

## Gaps and concerns

### 1. No pain data was recorded — the plan's progression gate is blind

The plan's decision rule is explicit: assess symptoms **during** (target 0–2/10),
**after** (should settle), and **the following morning** (same or better). Every
progression in the document is conditional on those readings.

No RPE and no notes were logged on any of the 20 sets. The session therefore
cannot answer the only question that determines what session 2 should look
like. This is the single most consequential gap, and it costs nothing to fix.

### 2. Every exercise was performed at the top of its rep range on day one

Ex 1, 2 and 3 hit 20 (ceiling of 15–20). Ex 4 and 5 hit 15 (ceiling of 12–15).
Ex 6 hit 10 (ceiling of the initial 8–10).

The plan is built as **graded exposure** — Stage 1 restores tolerance, Stage 2
adds repetitions. Starting at the ceiling collapses those two stages into
session one and leaves no runway: the only remaining progression is added
resistance, which is Stage 3.

This is a consequence of how the routine was built — it encoded "use the max of
the specified range", which set every target at ceiling. The rep ranges in the
plan are a progression ladder, not a target to max out.

The 14-rep third set on exercise 1 supports this. That is the plan's designated
*primary* cuff exercise, and it dropped below the prescribed floor at the end of
the session — a reasonable signal that 3 × 20/side is currently at or slightly
above capacity, and that 15 was the right starting point.

### 3. The bottoms-up carry missed on both dose and modality

Prescribed: **3 sets × 20–30 seconds per arm**. Logged: **2 sets × 20 metres**.

The exercise template is Hevy type `short_distance_weight`, so it can only
record distance — the time-based prescription cannot be logged against it. A
20-metre walk is roughly 20–25 seconds, so each set is about right, but at 2
sets this is roughly a third of the prescribed volume if the two sets were one
per arm.

This is the plan's **only** exercise for goal 4 (general shoulder stability),
so the shortfall lands entirely on one goal. A duration-type template would let
the prescription be logged as written.

### 4. Band resistance is not being captured

Exercises 5 and 6 logged at **0 kg**. The plan's progression for the
deceleration drill is explicit — 2×8 → 2×12 → 3×12 → 3×15, *then* increase
resistance and restart. Without a record of which band was used, "increase
resistance slightly" cannot be verified next session, and a change in band
would be invisible in the data.

### 5. The 20 kg on the serratus punch looks anomalous

Exercise 3 logged 20 kg — four times the load on the external rotation, on a
movement the plan describes as a 5–10 cm reach with a near-straight arm. This
is most likely a band's nominal rating rather than actual resistance, but if it
is a real load it is inconsistent with the plan's emphasis on control over
loading for serratus work. Worth confirming.

## Suggested adjustments for session 2

1. **Record a pain score.** Use Hevy's RPE field or a note on exercise 1 and
   exercise 6 — during, and again next morning. Nothing else in this list
   matters as much.
2. **Back the rep targets down into range** — ER at side to 3 × 15/side,
   deceleration drill to 2 × 8/side — and climb the ladder from there, rather
   than sitting at ceiling.
3. **Log band tension.** Even a nominal "red band = 10 kg" convention in the
   weight field makes exercises 5 and 6 progressible.
4. **Fix the carry**: 3 sets per arm, and a duration-type template so the
   20–30 second prescription can actually be recorded.
5. **Decide on Sword PULL.** It fits the plan's goals, but it added roughly 6
   minutes of working time to an already 49-minute session. The plan says the
   goal "is not to turn this into a hard bodybuilding shoulder workout."
