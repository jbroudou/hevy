# Boxing Shoulder Pain: Working Hypothesis and Prevention Plan

## Summary

After a week of boxing, both shoulders became painful internally, especially when lifting the arms directly out to the side.

The pattern so far is:

- Pain developed **after boxing sessions**, rather than during them.
- The shoulders tend to feel worse when first moving, then **ease as they warm up**.
- Raising the arm directly sideways with the **palm down causes about 5/10 pain**.
- Raising the arm directly sideways with the **thumb up causes no pain**.
- Raising the arm about 30° forward in the scapular plane with the **thumb up also causes no pain**.
- There has been no reported traumatic event, sudden loss of strength, or inability to raise the arms.

This pattern is most consistent with **load-sensitive rotator-cuff/subacromial irritation from boxing volume**, rather than clearly pointing to a major acute tear.

This is a working hypothesis, not a formal diagnosis.

  brakes elbow extension on a miss; the painless thumb-up tests make it less

## What May Be Causing It

One plausible contributor is **deceleration of missed punches**.

When a punch lands on a bag, pad, or opponent, the target helps absorb and slow the arm.

When a punch misses:

1. The arm has already been accelerated forward.
2. There is no target to absorb the momentum.
3. The arm wants to continue travelling forward.
4. The shoulder musculature has to actively slow and stabilise the arm.

The **posterior rotator cuff**, including muscles such as the infraspinatus and teres minor, contributes to controlling and decelerating the upper arm.

If boxing volume rises quickly, the cuff may be asked to perform this braking action hundreds of times before it has enough endurance for the workload.

Fatigue can therefore make the shoulder increasingly dependent on tissues that are already becoming overloaded.

The likely problem is therefore not simply:

> "My shoulders are weak."

It is more specifically:

> **The shoulder stabilisers and decelerators may not currently have enough strength and endurance for the amount and speed of punching being performed.**

Technique may also contribute, particularly if punches are repeatedly thrown into excessive end-range extension, but this program is designed to improve shoulder capacity even before technique changes are made.

---

# What We Are Trying to Improve

The program has four main goals.

## 1. Rotator-Cuff Endurance

The cuff must stabilise the shoulder repeatedly throughout a boxing session.

Rather than only building maximal strength, we want the cuff to tolerate **many contractions without fatiguing**.

This is why several exercises use:

- relatively light resistance;
- 12–20 repetitions;
- controlled eccentric phases.

---

## 2. Eccentric Deceleration Capacity

A muscle performs an **eccentric contraction** when it produces force while lengthening.

This is important when stopping a missed punch.

The arm is travelling forward, while the posterior shoulder muscles are working to slow that forward movement.

The training therefore includes exercises where the band tries to pull the arm forward and the shoulder must **slowly resist it**.

---

## 3. Shoulder-Blade Stability

The rotator cuff does not work independently.

The scapula must move and stabilise correctly on the rib cage to provide a good platform for the shoulder.

Important muscles include:

- serratus anterior;
- trapezius;
- rhomboids and other scapular stabilisers.

Rows, serratus work, and bottoms-up kettlebell carries are included for this reason.

---

## 4. General Shoulder Stability

Boxing involves rapid changes in direction and large forces travelling through the shoulder.

A **bottoms-up kettlebell carry** creates instability and forces the shoulder, wrist, forearm and scapular musculature to make continuous small corrections.

This complements conventional cuff strengthening.

---

# Current Shoulder-Conditioning Session

Perform approximately **2–3 times per week**, ideally with at least one day between sessions.

The goal is not to turn this into a hard bodybuilding shoulder workout.

The goal is **control, endurance and stability**.

## Warm-Up

### Easy Shoulder Movement and Shadowboxing

**2 minutes**

- Gentle arm circles.
- Easy shoulder movement.
- Very light shadowboxing.
- Gradually increase movement rather than starting with hard punches.

---

# 1. Band External Rotation — Elbow at Side

### Purpose

Primary rotator-cuff endurance exercise.

### Setup

- Anchor a resistance band beside you.
- Keep elbow bent to 90°.
- Keep elbow lightly against the ribs.
- Rotate the hand outward.

### Tempo

- Rotate outward: **1 second**
- Return inward: **3 seconds**

The slow return adds eccentric loading.

### Prescription

**3 sets × 15–20 reps per side**

**Rest: 30 seconds**

Use a light enough band that technique remains clean through the entire set.

---

# 2. Band or Cable Row

### Purpose

Build scapular and posterior shoulder endurance.

### Technique

- Pull toward the lower ribs.
- Avoid shrugging the shoulders toward the ears.
- Allow the shoulder blades to move naturally.
- Do not aggressively force them together.

### Prescription

**3 sets × 15–20 reps**

**Rest: 45 seconds**

---

# 3. Serratus Punch

### Purpose

Strengthen the serratus anterior and improve scapular control.

### Movement

This is **not really an arm punch**.

Start with the arm straight in front.

Keep the elbow almost completely straight.

Then:

1. Reach the fist a little farther forward.
2. Allow the shoulder blade to slide around the rib cage.
3. Return slowly.

The fist may only move around **5–10 cm**.

Think:

> "Keep my arm straight and reach for something just out of reach."

### Prescription

**2 sets × 15–20 reps per side**

**Rest: 30 seconds**

---

# 4. Thumb-Up Scaption Raise

### Why This Version

Testing showed:

- Palm-down side raise: **5/10 pain**
- Thumb-up side raise: **0/10**
- Thumb-up scaption: **0/10**

Therefore the pain-free thumb-up/scaption position is currently preferred.

### Technique

- Arms approximately 30° forward from directly sideways.
- Thumbs pointing upward.
- Raise to approximately shoulder height.
- Lower slowly.

### Tempo

- Up: **2 seconds**
- Down: **3 seconds**

### Prescription

**3 sets × 12–15 reps**

**Rest: 45 seconds**

Start with bodyweight or approximately **1–2 kg**, depending on comfort.

Avoid deliberately training the painful palm-down lateral-raise position for now.

---

# 5. External Rotation at 90°

### Purpose

Train the posterior rotator cuff in a more athletic shoulder position.

### Setup

Think of making half of a **goalpost or cactus position**:

- Upper arm approximately at shoulder height.
- Elbow bent 90°.
- Elbow stays in roughly the same position.

Start with the forearm pointing forward.

Rotate at the shoulder so the forearm moves upward/backward.

Then slowly return.

### Tempo

- Rotate backward/up: **1–2 seconds**
- Return forward: **3 seconds**

### Prescription

**2 sets × 12–15 reps per side**

**Rest: 45 seconds**

Use a very light band.

If simply placing the shoulder in this position reproduces the current shoulder pain, skip this exercise temporarily and continue with external rotation at the side instead.

---

# 6. Boxing Deceleration Drill

## Purpose

Train the shoulder to resist the forward movement that occurs when a punch misses.

### Setup

- Anchor a light resistance band **in front of you**.
- Face the anchor in your boxing stance.
- Hold the band in the punching hand.
- Start with the hand near the guard position.

### Movement

The band tries to pull the fist forward.

Instead of allowing it to snap the arm forward:

1. Slowly allow the fist to travel forward.
2. Resist the band throughout the movement.
3. Take approximately **3–4 seconds** to reach near-extension.
4. Stop at roughly **80–90% of normal punch extension**.
5. Do not lock the elbow.
6. Return to guard normally.

Think:

> **"The band wants to throw my fist forward. I am applying the brakes."**

### Prescription Initially

**2 sets × 8–10 reps per side**

**Rest: 45–60 seconds**

### Progression

Build gradually:

- 2 × 8
- 2 × 12
- 3 × 12
- 3 × 15

Once **3 × 15** can be performed with excellent control and without increased pain during, after, or the following morning, increase resistance slightly and reduce reps again.

The progression should generally be:

**control → repetitions → speed → resistance**

rather than immediately using a strong band.

Eventually the drill can become faster and more boxing-specific, because real punches occur quickly.

Slow eccentric work builds the capacity first.

---

# 7. Bottoms-Up Kettlebell Carry

### Purpose

Develop dynamic shoulder stability.

The upside-down kettlebell is unstable, forcing constant small corrections from:

- rotator cuff;
- serratus;
- scapular stabilisers;
- forearm;
- wrist.

### Setup

Hold the kettlebell upside down:

- bell above the fist;
- wrist straight;
- forearm approximately vertical;
- elbow bent;
- shoulder relaxed rather than shrugged.

Walk slowly while keeping the kettlebell balanced.

### Prescription

**3 sets × 20–30 seconds per arm**

**Rest: 45–60 seconds**

Start much lighter than a normal kettlebell press.

The goal is stability, not maximal loading.

Once **3 × 30 seconds** is easy and stable, move to the next sensible kettlebell weight.

---

# Optional Light Boxing Finisher

If the shoulders are comfortable:

**2 × 1 minute very light shadowboxing**

**Rest: 30 seconds**

Concentrate on:

- relaxed shoulders;
- controlled extension;
- returning to guard;
- not aggressively locking the elbow.

# Braking Capacity: Shoulder and Trunk

*Added 14 Sep 2026. Extends the program above; nothing in it replaces
exercises 1–7.*

## The idea in one line

The hips, trunk, chest, front shoulder and triceps are the engine of the
cross. The posterior rotator cuff is the brake. When a cross lands, the target
does most of the braking. When it misses, the brake has to stop the arm alone,
at full speed, in its most awkward position. A strong punch is only a problem
in the sense that a faster car needs bigger brakes. The pressing history in
this account (bench to 90 kg, push press 57.5 kg) says the engine is well
trained and the brakes are not. The fix is to build the brakes, not to punch
softer.

## Shoulder brakes

The braking exercises already in the program are 1 (external rotation at the
side), 5 (external rotation at 90°) and 6 (the deceleration drill). Exercise 6
is the direct one and must be done every session, rear hand first. Exercises 2
and 3 give the shoulder blade a stable base so the cuff is not braking on a
moving platform.

### Deceleration drill on a cable

A cable is an acceptable and probably better option for the slow version:

- Constant load through the range, so the weight can be logged and progressed
  (the band version has been logged as 0 kg, which makes progression
  invisible).
- Set the pulley at chest to shoulder height, face the stack in boxing stance,
  hold the handle in the punching hand, walk back until there is light tension
  at guard.
- Start at 2.5–5 kg. Let the handle pull the fist out along the cross line
  over 3–4 seconds to 80–90% extension, elbow soft, then return to guard in
  1–2 seconds.
- A band's tension rises toward extension, which is closer to a real punch
  where momentum peaks at the end. Keep the band for the faster stages (stage
  3 and 4); use the cable while the goal is slow control and measurable
  progression.

### Two additions

**Dumbbell punch-out negatives.** Lie on the back, light dumbbell (1–3 kg) in
the punching hand, fist at guard. Press the fist up and forward along the
cross line in 1 second, then lower it back to guard over 3–4 seconds while
keeping the shoulder blade flat on the floor. 2 × 10 per side. Gravity does
the accelerating; the slow lower is the brake.

**Guard-to-extension isometric hold.** Same setup as the deceleration drill.
Let the fist out to about 70% extension and hold it there against the band
or cable for 20–30 seconds, elbow soft. 2–3 holds per side. Builds the
endurance of the braking position without any movement to control.

### Sport-specific ladder (after stage 3)

1. Rear-hand shadowboxing with a deliberate stop short of lockout, 2 × 1 min.
2. Bag work where a few crosses per round are thrown past the bag on purpose,
   at half speed, then at speed.
3. Sparring rounds ramped from one to full, with the next-morning right-side
   scaption score deciding whether the previous step was tolerated.

Do not move up a rung if the next-morning score is worse than the day before.

## Trunk brakes

The same concept applies to the core, and it feeds the shoulder problem
directly. A cross starts at the rear foot, rotates through the hips and trunk,
and the arm is the last link. If the trunk does not stop its own rotation on a
miss, the arm reaches further, ends up more across the body, and the shoulder
takes a bigger share of the braking. The braking muscles are the obliques, the
deep abdominals and the muscles around the rear hip.

**Anti-rotation (resist being turned).**

- Pallof press, 3 × 10 per side, 2-second hold at full extension.
- Band-resisted rotation in boxing stance with a 3-second return, 2 × 10 per
  side.

**Catch and stop (produce rotation, then arrest it).**

- Medicine ball rotational throw against a wall, 3 × 6 per side. The catch is
  the point, not the throw: absorb the ball with the trunk and stop the
  rotation cleanly.
- Landmine rotation (the landmine 180 already in the log at 10 kg on 7 Sep).
  See the note below.

### What "emphasise the return phase" means on the landmine

In a landmine 180 the bar swings in an arc from one hip to the other. The
braking happens at the end of each arc, when the obliques on the far side stop
the bar and reverse it. Most people let the bar bounce off the bottom of the
arc and use that rebound to start the next rep, which trains the engine and
skips the brake. Instead:

- Slow the bar down over the last third of each arc rather than the last few
  centimetres.
- Stop it dead at the bottom, no bounce, and pause for one second.
- Only then start the next rep.
- Think of each arc as a cross and each stop as a miss.

At 10 kg this turns the exercise from a swing into a braking drill without
changing the load. Add weight only once the stop is clean at the current
weight.

Same progression as the shoulder work: control, then reps, then speed, then
load. All of this can sit in the same session as exercises 1–7, because none
of it is heavy.

# How to Progress the Whole Program

The long-term progression is:

## Stage 1 — Restore Tolerance

- Pain-free movement.
- Light cuff strengthening.
- High-quality repetitions.
- Slow eccentric control.

## Stage 2 — Build Endurance

- Increase repetitions.
- Increase time under tension.
- Improve bottoms-up carry duration.
- Maintain good shoulder mechanics under fatigue.

## Stage 3 — Increase Deceleration Demand

- Slightly stronger bands.
- More eccentric resistance.
- Greater control near punch extension.

## Stage 4 — Increase Speed

Once the shoulders tolerate the previous stages:

- perform deceleration drills progressively faster;
- gradually expose the cuff to more boxing-specific velocities.

The eventual objective is:

> **More resistance + more speed + excellent control**

rather than simply becoming strong with slow exercises.

While the shoulders are still irritated:

Temporarily reduce or avoid:

- high-volume hard bag work;
- hard missed punches;
- sparring rounds where the rear hand is thrown at full commitment
  (misses cannot be avoided in sparring, so this is the main dose to control);
- heavy overhead pressing;
- upright rows;
- painful palm-down lateral raises;
- repeatedly testing the painful movement.

Pain-free movements such as thumb-up scaption can continue.

Light shadowboxing may be acceptable if symptoms remain minimal during the session and do not worsen afterward.

# Pain Monitoring

A useful rule is to assess symptoms at three points.

## During Training

Preferably around **0–2/10**.

Mild discomfort may sometimes be acceptable during rehabilitation, but sharp or progressively increasing pain is not the objective.

## After Training

Symptoms should settle rather than steadily increase.

## The Following Morning

The shoulder should be approximately the same or better.

If an exercise causes a clear next-day increase in pain, reduce:

- resistance;
- repetitions;
- speed;
- range of motion;
- or overall training volume.

---

# Bigger Picture

The aim is not to make the rotator cuff impossible to injure.

Instead, the goal is to **raise the shoulder's workload capacity**.

If current shoulder capacity is below the demands of boxing:

**boxing workload > shoulder capacity → irritation**

Training aims to change this to:

**greater cuff strength + greater cuff endurance + better eccentric braking + better scapular stability → greater shoulder capacity**

Technique improvements may further reduce the load placed on the shoulder, particularly during missed punches.

The conditioning work is intended to make the shoulder much more resilient even before those technique improvements occur.

---

# When to Seek Assessment

A sports physiotherapist or doctor should assess the shoulder if there is:

- significant or worsening weakness;
- inability to raise the arm;
- sudden traumatic onset;
- major swelling or deformity;
- persistent numbness or tingling;
- increasingly severe night pain;
- or failure to improve despite reducing the aggravating workload.

The exercises above are a capacity-building strategy based on the current symptom pattern, not a substitute for an examination if symptoms persist or worsen.
