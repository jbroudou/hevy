## The working hypothesis [P]

The most likely explanation is load-sensitive rotator-cuff / subacromial
irritation from boxing volume, driven mainly by decelerating missed punches.
Refined 14 Sep (athlete): the trigger was a week of sparring, specifically
missing the opponent with right crosses. The right shoulder is the problem;
the left was affected but is now much milder. Pain tests:

- Palm-down side raise: 5/10
- Thumb-up side raise: 0/10
- Thumb-up scaption: 0/10
- Thumb-neutral scaption (14 Sep): 6/10 first few lifts, 3/10 after that

The last result fits the same movement pattern (pain in neutral / internal
rotation, none with the thumb up). A warm-up response can occur with
load-sensitive tendon pain, but does not rule out a partial tear or another
cause. The rapid return plan replaces the deliberately painful daily scaption
test with symptom logging during ordinary movement. The original plan has a
"Braking Capacity" section covering a cable version of the deceleration drill,
two extra braking exercises, trunk anti-rotation and catch-and-stop work, and a
sparring re-entry ladder.

This is a working hypothesis, not a diagnosis. The plan has four goals: cuff
endurance, eccentric deceleration, scapular stability and general shoulder
stability.

### Plyometric and agility work (advised 18 Sep)

Lower-body plyometrics and agility drills do not load the shoulder and are
permitted; upper-body plyometrics in the routines (medicine-ball chest pass,
ball slams, clap push-ups) stay out. Evidence checked 18 Sep: Loturco et al.
2016 (15 Brazilian national-team boxers) found jump performance strongly
correlated with punch impact — CMJ r = 0.72 jab / 0.80 cross, loaded jump squat
0.83–0.85; correlational only. A systematic review of agility-ladder training
finds consistent gains in change of direction and coordination, best when
combined with plyometrics or multidirectional speed work and with a reactive
element. (An initial assumption that ladder work does not transfer was wrong.)

- **Placement:** first in the strength sessions, fresh — never after the 4×4.
  Session B's hurdle jumps moved from fourth to first accordingly. After
  conditioning, only easy footwork; low-amplitude ladder/cone patterns may
  serve as that footwork content at the same dose.
- **Dose:** ~50 foot contacts a week to start, building ~10% a week.
- **Calf rule:** stuck-landing jumps now (box, hurdle, broad, lateral
  bound-and-hold). Rebound work — pogos, continuous bounds, depth jumps,
  skipping rope — only after the calf/soleus routine has 5–6 sessions logged
  (one as of 18 Sep). Rope is also conditional on the shoulder at 0–2/10.
- **P2D1** is an all-lower-body routine and shoulder-safe as written, but runs
  ~108 contacts including depth jumps: use at half jump volume with box jumps
  substituted for depth jumps.

Sources: [Loturco et al. 2016](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26110348/) ·
[Agility ladder drills: a systematic review](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/396254042_Agility_ladder_drills_in_sports_training_a_systematic_review) ·
[Ladder plus plyometric / multidirectional drills in volleyball](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39326861/)
