## Current prescription

Revised 13 Sep, when the athlete asked whether to keep running as at present
or split into a slow run plus a hard assault-bike session, with 2–3 cardio
sessions a week available. Answer: split. [P, C, I]

Reasoning: the four runs to date all sit in the grey zone (19–29 min Threshold,
7–13 min Aerobic Endurance, no intervals), which builds neither the base nor the
ceiling efficiently and is costing recovery (load per Z2 minute 3.8 → 24.5,
resting HR 56 → 59). Sparring needs both ends. The bike removes impact from the
hard session — the calf/Achilles risk is the main injury exposure — and keeps
the legs fresher for boxing.

| Sessions/week | Easy | Hard | Third |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 2 | Zone 2, 45–60 min, capped at 142 | Bike intervals | — |
| 3 | same | same | Second Zone 2 (preferred) or repeated-sprint bike |

**Sparring replaces the hard session; it does not add to it.** [P]

*Easy session.* Range 126–141, settle around 130–138, hard cap 142 (slow or
walk until back under). **40 min now, +5 min/week: 40 → 45 → 50 → 60.** Expect
roughly 6:30–7:00 /km [I] — on 3 Sep, 6:01 /km held ~137 for only 15 min
before drifting. Incline-walk alternative (gradient 6 / 6 km/h) can go 45–60
min sooner at lower leg cost. Given 4 of 4 runs overshot and only the 2 Sep walk
respected the ceiling, the walk is the safer choice until an easy run has
proven it stays easy.

*Hard session: Norwegian 4×4* (athlete's choice, 13 Sep). Helgerud et al.
2007: 4 × 4 min at 90–95% max HR, 3 min active recovery at ~70%, 10 min
warm-up; ~7% VO2max gain in 8 weeks, while the continuous-at-threshold group
barely changed. **For max HR 179: work 161–170, recovery ~125.** Total ~36–40
min. Rules: reach the target by the end of each rep, not from the start (HR
lags — rep 1 may take 3 min); even pace across all four reps; recovery is a
jog/spin, not standing; once a week, and **sparring replaces it**. Progress by
pace or watts per rep at the same HR.

Where: **bike, legs-only while the shoulder is irritated** (bike HR runs
lower — RPE 8–9 at 155–160 is acceptable); treadmill at the study's 5.3%
incline once the calf routine has a few more weeks behind it; outdoors is the
highest-impact option.

Control the hard session by RPE and watts/calories per rep, not HR (bike HR
runs lower and lags on short reps). HR cap remains the control for the easy
session.

*Evidence basis (checked 13 Sep against the papers, not recalled):*

- Threshold-dominant distributions are consistently the weakest for VO2peak;
  both polarised and pyramidal beat them, and both put most volume at low
  intensity. Stöggl & Sperlich 2014 (POL +11.7% VO2peak, THR n.s.); Rosenblat
  2019 (3–4 RCTs, ES −0.66 for TT, quality 4–5/10); Silva Oliveira 2024 (17
  studies, VO2peak SMD 0.24, larger in <12-week blocks, no TT difference);
  Rosenblat 2025 network MA (POL ≈ pyramidal; recreational athletes did
  better with pyramidal). **Magnitude small-to-moderate; populations are
  endurance athletes, not boxers.**
- HIIT added to combat training raises VO2max modestly: Vasconcelos 2020
  (+2.8 ml/kg/min in strikers); Franchini 2019 (+4.4–23% across 9 studies).
- Bike vs run: no evidence of a meaningful transfer difference (Frontiers 2026
  MA, 7 old RCTs, n.s.). **The bike choice is a risk call on the calf/Achilles
  and shoulder, not a literature finding.** Running intervals are equally
  supported.
- No study compares grey-zone running against easy-plus-HIIT in boxers. The
  strongest direct evidence is this athlete's own four-run trend.

*Superseded:* the 10 Sep two-run prescription (running intervals 5 × 3 / 1 plus
an easy run). Running intervals remain an option if no bike is available, at
